# Topic depth and teaching-quality gate

A topic is not considered covered merely because its name appears or it links to a lesson. Every canonical topic must pass all mandatory checks below.

## Mandatory teaching components

1. **Plain-language foundation** — defines the concept and unfamiliar terms without assuming expert knowledge.
2. **Mechanism and value** — explains how it works, why it improves Claude Code work, and what problem it solves.
3. **Use boundaries** — states prerequisites, when to use it, and when not to use it.
4. **Reproducible procedure** — provides at least four concrete, ordered actions where the concept is procedural.
5. **Worked application** — includes a realistic scenario with inputs, actions, and expected evidence or output.
6. **Contrast** — shows a specific weak approach and an improved approach, then explains the meaningful difference.
7. **Failure handling** — identifies at least two credible failure modes and gives a recovery action for each.
8. **Reusable aid** — includes a prompt, command, checklist, decision rule, or template that the learner can apply.
9. **Practice and mastery** — includes an exercise plus an observable success criterion or answer explanation.
10. **Traceable evidence** — cites at least one supplied PDF and page; synthesis or inference is explicitly labeled.
11. **Visual treatment** — uses and explains a relevant source screenshot/diagram when one materially helps; otherwise records why a visual is not applicable.
12. **Progressive depth** — separates the beginner explanation from advanced nuance instead of compressing both into one paragraph.

## Quantitative safeguards

- At least 350 instructional words per canonical topic, excluding navigation, raw PDF text, citation labels, and repeated boilerplate.
- At least one concrete example and one reusable learning aid.
- At least four procedure steps when `procedural: true`.
- At least two failure-and-recovery pairs.
- At least one page-level citation for source-supported guidance.
- No unresolved placeholder, unsupported factual claim, broken citation target, or missing referenced asset.

These thresholds are safeguards, not substitutes for editorial judgment. A lesson may meet the counts and still fail if it is vague, repetitive, inaccurate, or not reproducible.
